Akbar Shirini, Shen Xudong, Zhao Dan School of Oriental Studies, Jilin International Studies University, Changchun, China akshirini@jisu.edu.cn Abstract
This paper explores the symbolic representation of musk in classical Persian poetry, uncovering its role as a multifaceted emblem of Iranian cultural identity and human experience. Drawing on a qualitative analysis of primary texts from the Ganjoor archive and secondary sources, the study traces musk’s historical significance across China, Iran, and Islamic world. In China, musk is prized for its rarity, medicinal properties, and metaphysical roles; in Iran, it intertwines with mythology, Zoroastrian cosmology, and trade; in Islamic contexts, it holds commercial and therapeutic value, cherished by figures like Mohammad. Within Persian poetry, musk transcends its material origins, symbolizing blackness (beauty and aging), resilience (blossoming despite adversity), seclusion (spiritual introspection), chastity (purity), enduring friendship (eternal bonds), homeland yearning (nostalgia), cosmetics (self-presentation), cuisine (vitality), and wealth (prestige).
These themes reflect poets’ navigation of socio-historical tensions – balancing patronage with reformist critique – while bridging the mundane and transcendent. Musk’s symbolic density offers insights into evolving social norms, Sufi ideals, and emotional landscapes, positioning it as a reflection of aspects of Iranian cultural heritage.
Keywords: Musk, Persian poetry, symbolism, Iranian culture, olfactory imagery Significance Statement
Ancient societies immortalized aesthetic ideals through diverse media, from sculpture to poetry (Da Soller 2010). The human drive to enhance attractiveness via fragrance transcends cultures and classes, as shown by resource allocations to this end (Williams and Apicella 2023; Beaton, et al. 2022). Modern research confirms that olfactory stimuli shape perception through mood, priming, and arousal (Spence 2020, 2021), with scents altering subjective experience across sensory domains (Lorimer 2023; Foster 2008).
Acknowledging the pivotal role of fragrance in shaping Iranian cultural and literary expression, this study undertakes a narrative of musk within the textual corpus of classical Persian poetry. Exploring musk’s symbolism in Persian culture, we aim to uncover its unique meanings beyond empirical effects on perception or status.
## Introduction
Literary analyses meticulously dissect the representation of social realities within poetry, examining its historical role as both a reflective surface and an instigator of societal transformation.
For Iranians, classical poetry serves as a vital repository of each era's prevailing spirit, a distilled essence of the zeitgeist (Bina, et al. 2020); even quotidian experiences, when rendered through verse, offer profound insights into the human condition and its attendant emotional landscape (Malmir and Mohebi 2015).
In Iran, poets were historically tasked with the critical examination of societal ills, the articulation of popular aspirations and anxieties, and the illumination of prevailing preoccupations (Ashtiany 2020). Moreover, it serves as a conduit for Sufi philosophical thought, promoting social harmony, spiritual discipline, and the pursuit of inner peace and enlightenment (Omidali and Soltani 2025). Furthermore, the art of classical verse cultivates empathy and demonstrates the potent efficacy of succinct expression and concentrated language, rendering it an indispensable tool for understanding the Iranian historical and cultural narrative (Aydenloo 2023, 21).
One of the most pervasive aspects of daily life, extensively represented within classical Persian literature, is the utilization of aromatic substances. Recognizing the potent influence of olfactory stimuli on human attraction, a phenomenon validated by longstanding traditional knowledge, individuals have consistently sought to optimize their appeal within the social arena, driven by the pressures of both interpersonal and intrapersonal competition (Spence 2021). Thus, the strategic manipulation of scent, through the application of perfumes, emerged as a significant form of artistic expression, analogous to poetry or painting.
The art of perfumery, from its inception, served as a powerful tool for individuals to navigate and influence their social landscape (Kowal, et al. 2024). Along the same lines, Persian poets were keenly aware of fragrance’s power. The extensive corpus of Persian words and phrases derived from 'musk' attests to its esteemed position as the quintessential aromatic substance within Persian culture (Anoosheh 1996, 257b). The annals of Persian literature reveal that many poets, constrained by the exigencies of their time, sought patronage within royal courts or among the affluent elite.
The crux of the matter lies in the poets' response and agency within their specific sociohistorical context (Eydgah and Abadian 2011); On one hand, the pressure exerted by the ruling minority to represent and solidify the regime's desired ideology and values, and on the other, the poets' social mandate and commitment to advocating for reforms, engender a discursive formation (Parsa Nasab 2022, 51).
The poets, therefore, navigate a complex terrain of ideological negotiation, employing rhetorical strategies that allow for a degree of compliance with the dominant discourse while subtly embedding critiques and calls for social change (Mozaffarian 2012). In the face of these unprecedented circumstances, Persian poets employed musk, a component of courtly life and wealth, to articulate social concerns and daily life.
Persian poetry has consistently endeavored to maintain its preeminence and authority in social mediation. Poets strategically utilized indirect language, ambiguity, and symbolism to secure their desired outcomes. In Persian poetry, symbolism is a dynamic instrument for articulating complex human experiences across temporal and cultural divides.
Symbolic expression, fundamentally, embodies humanity's quest to apprehend and materialize concepts that transcend the tangible world, thereby functioning as a catalyst for intellectual contemplation (Nehmé, et al. 2016). This inherent capacity renders symbolism an indispensable component of human cognition, serving both to satisfy psychological and emotional needs and to articulate abstract ideas that defy conventional linguistic expression.
Furthermore, the interpretation of symbols, as demonstrated in Persian, necessitates a nuanced understanding that surpasses ethnic and temporal boundaries, demanding intellectual maturity, profound imagination, and astute poetic contemplation. (Fereydooni and Razaghpoor 2023). Symbolic framing elevates narrative from mere communication to an artistic medium, enriching thematic depth and revealing sociocultural realities.
The strategic utilization of symbolism, therefore, serves to deepen poetic expression, liberating it from superficiality and opening a multiplicity of interpretive avenues for the reader (Pedersen 2015).
This process of meaning discovery transforms poetry into a dynamic space for intellectual and emotional engagement, highlighting the intricate interplay between poetic expression, symbolic depth, and socio-cultural commentary within Persian literature (Shirini, Shen, and Zhao 2025).
This study goes beyond empirical effects on perception or status to explore the poeticcultural significations of musk. Thus, the present study asks: what symbolic roles does musk play in classical Persian poetry, and what do these roles reveal about the sociocultural and spiritual landscape of the era?
## Outline
In the sections that follow, Methodology first details the qualitative, text-based approach, combining close readings of primary poetic sources from the Ganjoor archive with secondary historical analyses to categorize musk’s symbolic functions. This study will then review the literature investigating the advent and evolution of musk across three Asian regions.
Firstly, an analysis of China will be presented, focusing on historical evidence of musk's medicinal, metaphysical, and sociocultural roles. Secondly, Persian literature will be explored, examining musk conceptualization within legends, Zoroastrian cosmology, royal courts, and tribute systems. Finally, the introduction and early use of musk in Western Asia and the medieval Middle East will be traced, charting its evolution from rarity to significance in pharmacology, perfumery, and social practice. This trajectory reveals how trade, religion, and scientific inquiry collectively shaped musk's reception and application in some Muslim countries.
The Discussion analyzes musk’s thematic representations in Persian poetry, such as blackness, resilience, seclusion, chastity, friendship, nostalgia, cosmetics, cuisine, and wealth, revealing its role in reflecting socio-historical tensions and human experiences.
The Conclusion synthesizes these findings, emphasizing musk’s transformation into a versatile symbol that bridges the mundane and transcendent, while suggesting avenues for future ecological and cultural research.
## Methodology
This study employed a qualitative, text-based approach to investigate the symbolic functions of musk in classical Persian poetry, drawing on both primary literary sources and secondary scholarly analyses. Classical Persian poetics is defined by its rigorous adherence to the ‘arūḍ system. This tradition prioritizes the systematic organization of prosodic elements, including meter, rhyme, and fixed poetic forms.
This study does not limit its scope to a specific historical period but rather examines the works of all poets who have composed within the framework of classical Persian poetry. The primary corpus was accessed through the Ganjoor application, a comprehensive digital archive of major Persian literary works, including the poetry of Hafez, Saadi, Rumi, and Ferdowsi, among others.
Using this tool, a systematic search was conducted for the term mushk (musk) and its lexical variants (e.g., mushk-e nāfeh [navel musk], mushkīn [musk-like]), as well as related phrases (e.g., bū-ye mushk [scent of musk]). This search identified recurring instances of musk across poetic texts, enabling the recognition of its main thematic applications–such as sensuality, divinity, paradise, and social prestige–within their contextual settings.
To complement this primary analysis, secondary sources were consulted, including academic papers in Persian and English retrieved via search engines (e.g., Google Scholar) and library databases (especially www.sid.ir). These studies provided historical, cultural, and linguistic insights into musk’s significance in Persian contexts, from its Zoroastrian symbolism to its role in courtly patronage.
The methodology thus combined close reading of poetic texts with historical contextualization, aiming to delineate musk’s symbolic evolution beyond its material uses. To enhance interpretive validity, thematic analysis was complemented by crossreferencing with cultural histories. This dual approach bridged literary criticism and cultural studies and allowed us to identify common symbolic themes of musk across different poets and times.
## Review of literature
Human bodily emanations, rather than being mere olfactory noise, have evolved into a sophisticated chemosensory language (Hörberg, Kurfali and Olofsson 2025). These signals, therefore, provide crucial insights into an individual's physiological well-being (Shirasu and Touhara 2011), reproductive status (Singh and Bronstad 2001), and personality traits (Sorokowska, Sorokowski and Havlíček 2016).
Musk, a precious zoogenous medicinal material in traditional Chinese medicine, is primarily found in the urine of mature male deer belonging to the species Moschus berezovskii Flerov, Moschus sifanicus Przewalski, or Moschus moschiferus Linnaeus, likely serving as a territorial marker (King 2017, 15; Bian, et al. 2025; Feng, et al. 2023). The secretion, initially yellowish and milky, accumulates within the musk gland (Yu 2007). Over a period of approximately one month, it undergoes a maturation process, transitioning to a reddish-brown hue. Further darkening occurs upon removal from the animal, either through killing it or excising the gland while it is alive. The historical distribution of musk deer encompassed a broad expanse of forested and semi-forested highlands across Eastern Eurasia (Vislobokova and Lavrov 2009).
The English word “musk” is part of a large group of words used in the Middle East and in Europe that originates in Persian. Some of these words include Pahlavi mušk (spelled mwšk), Classical and New Persian mushk, Arabic misk (Jeffrey 1938), Armenian mušk (Hübschmann 1962), Syriac mušāk (Payne Smith 2007), Greek μόσχος (móskhos) (Brust 2005), Latin muscus (Jerome 1883), Spanish and Italian musco, muschio (Cueva 2017), French musc (Dauzat 1938) and German Moschus (Kluge 2012).

## Musk in China
China's historical preeminence in artisanal production established a foundation for the widespread adoption and adaptation of aromatic substances, including musk (Zhang, et al. 2024). This preeminence, coupled with extensive cultural and commercial networks, positions China as a pivotal site for the earliest documented use of musk (He, et al. 2024; Guo, Zhang, and Huang 2023).
Musk, prized for its rarity, singular efficacy, and pervasive market demand, coupled with its profound cultural significance, has consistently held a position of exceptional esteem within Chinese society. The earliest extant lexicographical reference to musk appears in the Erya (爾雅), potentially dating to the third century BCE, which identifies the musk deer as shefu (麝父) (Erya, n.d., Lower Scroll, 29b-30a). Tao Hongjing further substantiates this early documentation (陶弘景) (452–536), a seminal figure in early Chinese pharmacology, who provided detailed accounts of musk's medicinal applications (Unschuld 1985, 13).
These early records underscore not only the presence of musk, but also its integration into both practical and theoretical knowledge systems (Liu, et al. 2021).
Musk is historically recognized for its potent therapeutic properties. Classical texts emphasize its capacity to disperse obstructions in the orifices, enliven the spirit, and facilitate blood circulation, thereby alleviating menstrual blockages (Shuquan, et al. 2022) and clean the teeth and freshen the breath (Budge 1976). Furthermore, historical records document its application as an antivenom (Arora, Seth, and Somani 1962) and insect repellent (Al-Qazwīnī 1280, 326), highlighting its diverse pharmacological utility.
Modern scientific investigations have corroborated and expanded upon the historical understanding of musk's therapeutic potential.
Contemporary studies affirm its aromatic qualities, which are believed to invigorate the body, promote blood circulation, clear meridians, diminish swelling, and alleviate pain (Zhang, et al. 2020; Han, et al. 2022). In clinical settings, musk is employed to address a wide range of conditions, including fever, coma, stroke, phlegm accumulation, qi stagnation, menstrual disorders, chest pain, abdominal pain, numbness, and various deficiency and traumatic syndromes (Jinghua, et al. 2020; Naihua, et al. 2024). The consistent application of musk across historical and contemporary medical practices underscores its enduring significance as a therapeutic agent, demonstrating a clear causal relationship between traditional observations and modern clinical applications.
Beyond its medicinal applications, musk held significant metaphysical importance. Since antiquity, its transcendental and otherworldly functions have been a subject of scholarly inquiry. Beliefs persisted that musk facilitated communication with the divine and ancestral spirits (Taiping Yulan (太平御覽) 1960, 21). Historical records also attest to its utilization in sorcery and the performance of extraordinary feats (Li 2022, 51.39), suggesting its role in ritual and esoteric practices.
Within the socio-cultural sphere, musk perfume functioned as a potent symbolic marker of female identity and sexuality during early Chinese periods (Sikka and Bartolome 2018; Mostafa, El Khouly and Hassan 2011). Women employed musk to scent their persons, with the lingering olfactory trace on a lover serving as a tangible mnemonic of past sexual liaisons (Hightower 1973). Moreover, its use as a cosmetic for aesthetic enhancement is documented from at least the sixth century (Hightower 1959, 88), highlighting its multifaceted applications in personal adornment.
In China and East Asian cultures, musk was also integral to incense rituals, burned in temples, shrines, and domestic spaces (Gorman 1975), reflecting its spiritual and social significance. Furthermore, the literati valued musk in the creation of fine calligraphy inks, imparting a smooth consistency, aromatic quality, and deep black hue (Laufer 1926). This application underscores musk's integration into elite cultural practices. Finally, intellectual games centered on the identification of musk in complex blends (Cheung 2020; Horiguchi and Jung 2013), suggesting a sophisticated level of sensory discrimination and knowledge surrounding the substance.
## Musk in Iran
Lilja (1972) attributes the genesis of aromatic substances to the Persian civilization and dates their introduction to the Greeks to the expedition of Alexander (356-323 BCE).
Iranian mythology holds that Jamshīd, the legendary ancient king who introduced many amenities of civilized life to the world, initiated the use of aromatics:
He brought us scents, a fragrant, sweet supply, A need of all, beneath the boundless sky. From camphor's balm to musk's alluring grace, From pistachio's oil to incense's trace, Rosewater's bloom and anise's gentle art, He filled our world with fragrance, from the start (Ferdowsi 2009, 43)
Within the Zoroastrian cosmogony, olfactory perception held profound symbolic weight, particularly in eschatological contexts. Post-mortem, the soul's ethical conduct was allegorically represented through a female personification of its deeds. A woman of surpassing beauty thus depicted a virtuous life, while conversely, malevolent actions were embodied by a figure of corresponding ugliness, the degree of which reflected the magnitude of the transgressions:
On the third day at dawn, the soul of the righteous went about among the sweet-smelling plants, and to him that scent seemed more pleasant than all the sweet smells that had come into his nostrils, and that scented breeze came from the southern direction, from the region of God. Then came forward his own religion and his own deeds in the form of a girl in appearance, well grown, and grown in virtue, […] whose body was as brilliant as her appearance was most pleasing and her looks most fitting (Vahman 1986, 194-5).
In the case of the evil person, the soul appears as follows:
Then a cold stinking wind came to meet him. He had not experienced a fouler wind than that in the world. And in that wind he saw his own religion and deeds [in the form of] a naked whore, rotten, filthy, with crooked knees, with projecting buttocks, resembling the most hideous reptile, most filthy and most stinking (Vahman 1986, 201).
In the eschatological narratives of the Parthians and Sassanians, the resurrected bodies of the devout were imbued with celestial fragrances, a testament to their blessedness. For example, the Zoroastrian writer Zādsparam believes: “The humors in their bodies will be more fragrant than musk, ambergris, or camphor” (Zādspram 1993). Even the literary texts of that time like Ayādgār-ī Jāmāspīg, a work based on the Pahlavi Jāmāsp Nāmag, pure musk was associated with China: “And China has large cities, much gold, much musk, and many jewels” (Bailey 1932).
Al-Jāḥiẓ, in his ninth-century writings, reveals that the Sasanian kings received tributes from their magnates, each gift reflecting the unique expertise of their domains. Yemen, renowned for its fragrant riches, proffered musk and ambergris as its sovereign offering (Al-Jāḥiẓ 1914, 146-7). The Parthian and Sasanian empires appear to have played a significant role in transporting aromatics from East of Asia to Classical Mediterranean (Jones 2018). This imagery of scent is paralleled in Christian literature and later adopted in the Qurʾān and other early Islamic literature (King 2017, 124).
Within the hierarchy of musk, the Tibetan variety reigns supreme, followed by Sogdian, Chinese, and Indian (Yaghoobi 1963, 147). Persian poets have long celebrated the dark, lustrous quality of fine musk and its associated exorbitant price:
To grasp musk’s price, a treasure dark and deep Entrust the wind with hair, where shadows creep (Sanaei 2002, 819).
Night's sable cloak, a musky, darkened hue, Enveloped earth, where battles ceased to brew (Ferdowsi 2009, 563).
## Musk in West Asia and Islamic World
The initial awareness of aromatic products from Southeast Asia, including those that would later be associated with musk, arrived in Western Asia through maritime trade and interactions with the Sasanian Empire (Hassan and Tibbetts 1981). Early Arabic sources highlight the significance of these aromatics, particularly within pharmacological contexts (Miquel 1975, 35-37). This foundation is crucial for understanding the subsequent integration of musk into medical and perfumery practices.
The earliest documented references to musk reveal a gradual process of familiarization. An early Arabic commercial document, tentatively dated to the second century CE, suggests musk’s presence (Ryckmans, Müller and Abdallah 1994, 65). Further evidence from lexical analysis of Babylonian, Hebrew, and Arabic texts from the late third century, particularly the adoption of Persian-derived terms, points to a common transmission route and increasing familiarity with musk (Müller 1997, 209). This contrasts with the absence of musk in classical Greek medicine (Dalby 2000, 47), highlighting its specific introduction through Eastern trade routes (Yang 2016).
The sixth century witnessed the growing commercial appeal of musk, with Roman merchants recognizing its value in nascent maritime trade with India and Ceylon (Warmington 1974; McCabe 2009). This is supported by early Arabic literary evidence, such as the verse from al-Aʻs̆ â (c. 565-c. 629), which demonstrates the early recognition of its distinctive scent (1928, 6.13).
The high esteem in which musk was held is further substantiated by its association with prominent figures in early Islamic history. Documentary evidence suggests that Abū Ṭālib, the paternal uncle of Mohammad, traded perfumes, and that Mohammad valued aromatic substances, particularly musk (Goeje 2013).
The aphorism attributed to Umar al-Khaṭṭāb, the second caliph (c. 634–644), underscores the economic and sensory value of musk: “If I were a merchant, I would choose nothing but musk. Even if its profit escaped me, its scent would not” (Ghazūlī 2000, 1.75). The enigmatic animal origin of musk sparked scientific curiosity and speculation among Arab scholars (Akasoy and Yoeli-Tlalim 2007). This led to various hypotheses, including the belief that it originated from spikenard (Al-Qazwīnī 1280, 386), or through human intervention (King 2017, 214).
The emphasis on distinguishing between authentic and adulterated musk (Ibn-Isḥāq 1993, 226-232), and the preference for musk within the pod (Ibn Kaysān 1953, 190), highlight the development of sophisticated commercial practices and quality control.
The systematic theorization of aromatherapy began from the eighth century, with AlKindi (c. 796-873) playing a pivotal role in analyzing the psychological effects of perfumes and codifying their preparation (Inoue 2017). Musk, in particular, became an integral part of social and personal grooming, with distinct applications for men and women (Al-Shayzari 1999; Al-Washshāʾ 1886, 187). Furthermore, the interconnectedness of gastronomy, medicine, and perfumery is evident in the stringent pharmaceutical standards applied to musk. Its use as an antidote and therapeutic agent for neurological disorders demonstrates its integration into medical practice (Amar and Lev 2017).
In summary, musk’s status as a rare and precious substance, revered for medicinal, spiritual, and economic value across China, Iran, and the Islamic world, established a rich set of connotations that Persian poets could draw upon in their symbolic repertoire.
## Discussion
Persian poets were aware of musk's medicinal properties. Musk was traditionally employed as an antidote, a restorative for ailing individuals, an air purifier, a fever reducer, a remedy for colds, a cognitive stimulant, and a treatment for ocular ailments:
A whiff of musk, a glance at silken scrolls, Both gifts of China, sharpen mind and soul (Nezami 2002, 371).
Before my wounded heart succumbs to blight, Let Chinese musk, a cure, restore my sight (Saeb 1996, 195)
The analysis of the poetry reveals several recurring symbolic motifs attached to musk. These motifs, each explored below, range from physical attributes like color to abstract concepts like spiritual longing.
## Blackness
In Persian literature, the concept of blackness, often symbolized by elements such as the dark basal blotch of tulips, the human eye, eyebrow, hair, night sky, nimbostratus clouds, and even written and sketched forms, is frequently intertwined with the imagery of quality musk:
With musk (which is black), redesign not the flower’s grace, Nor veil the moon with that perfumed embrace (Sanaei 2002, 986).
A royal missive, from the Indian king, Inscribed in musk (which is black), on Chinese paper's wing (Ferdowsi 2009, 622).
The Persian idiom "to change from musk to camphor" (mushk beh kāfūr tabdil shudan), which symbolically equates aging with the transformation from the dark, fragrant musk to the white, pungent camphor, provides a vivid illustration of the cultural associations of these aromatics and their colors (Anoosheh 1996, 260b):
When silver threads within the hair take hold, A shadow falls, and joy begins to fold The musk that fades, to camphor turns its grace, And crowns adorn with less becoming face (Ferdowsi 2009, 16).
The graying of one's hair is often interpreted as a harbinger of diminished hope and future happiness. This metaphorical association suggests that the process of aging, symbolized by the whitening of hair, can lead to a decline in one's outlook on life. Similarly, the transformation of musk into camphor is understood as a loss of potency and desirability. This analogy highlights how a change in substance, from a highly valued aromatic to a more common one, can diminish the overall value or appeal of an object, even if it is placed in a setting of great beauty.
In Persian symbolism, whiteness transcends mere visual perception, embodying a multifaceted array of abstract concepts. It signifies ablution and spiritual purification, thus connoting the eradication of both external and internal malevolence.
Furthermore, whiteness is associated with knowledge, thereby acting as an antithesis to ignorance.
This symbolic association extends to encompass positive attributes such as sunshine and good fortune, as well as culturally noteworthy events like marriage. Moreover, whiteness is linked to tangible substances such as milk, flour, and silver.
## Blossom Despite Adversity
The transmutation of blood into precious musk, as posited within Persian literary tradition, serves as a potent symbolism for the cultivation of self-efficacy and personal actualization:
to musk from blood, no alchemy’s art avails, unless through austere trials, spirit sails. Nor can base cloth, though cunningly entwined, a noble raiment for true worth unwind (Saeb 1996, 1623).
Musk has become symbolically imbued with the values of self-regulation and psychological resilience. Recognizing the multifaceted nature of success, which encompasses biological, emotional, social, and cognitive determinants, Persian poets consistently underscore the necessity of both perseverance and focused exertion. They posit that the narrative of musk's creation functions as a didactic tool, encouraging individuals to foster a robust sense of agency, specifically, the belief in their capacity to orchestrate action sequences and sustain effort towards goal attainment.
In the same vein, poets advocate for the development of resilience and conscientiousness, crucial traits for navigating adversity, effectively self-regulating behavior, and maintaining adaptive functioning under challenging circumstances. They assert that the fruits of this transformative process, analogous to the creation of musk, directly correlate with enhanced psychological well-being, improved mental and physical health, occupational performance, and effective parenting:
Though musk’s abode is hidden, deep and still, Its scent ascends, supreme, to heaven’s hill (Nezami 2011, 283).
Humanity, a dual scent, A fragrant bloom, a foul event Garlic's scent, a pungent guise, Musk's allure, a sweet disguise (Molana 1984, 556).
## Seclusion
The confinement of musk within the restricted and darkened musk gland has, within Persian literary tradition, rendered it a potent symbol of solitude and spiritual introspection.
Aligning with the Persian literary inclination towards symbolic representation, where tangible phenomena are transmuted into abstract concepts, musk's seclusion signifies the introspective journey of self-cultivation and transcendental elevation.
This symbolism rests upon the belief that the quintessential essences of both the material and spiritual realms can be encapsulated within a minute locus, awaiting a transformative rebirth that facilitates their re-emergence into the external world. Therefore, solitude is construed as a liminal space wherein individuals can engage in a profound contemplation of the world's image reflected within the soul, and reciprocally, the soul's reflection within the world:
As musk, from solitude I’ve gained my scent, Like eagle’s soar, alone, content (Nezami 2011, 24).
Furthermore, it is posited that human existence is imbued with a celestial telos, achievable through the renunciation of personal caprice and the pursuit of a spiritual pilgrimage culminating in personal resurrection. Poets advocate for immersion within the metaphorical "ocean of solitude", a space conducive to meditation and the pursuit of ontological truths, thereby unlocking channels of divine grace, illumination, and revelation. This transformative process, analogous to the genesis of musk, necessitates self-abnegation, sacrifice, and the endurance of suffering.
In essence, solitude, within this framework, serves as a crucible for psychological and spiritual metamorphosis, fostering self-actualization through a process of introversion and existential reflection.
## Chastity
The genesis of musk, attributed to an alchemical transmutation, has imbued it with symbolic connotations of chastity and purity, thereby establishing it as a representation of untainted essence within Persian literature. This symbolism, often articulated as "the odor of chastity," extends to encompass a constellation of related virtues, including loyalty, constancy, temperance, modesty, and piety. These virtues, cultivated through profound reflection and sustained effort, contribute to a depth of spiritual penetration:
a maiden pure, than musk more bright, still loves me true, through darkest night (Nezami 2006, 75)
Moreover, Persian literary and philosophical discourse emphasizes chastity as an acquired virtue, transcending biological determinism and gendered limitations. This cultivated virtue is presented as a conduit to transcendence, a "ladder to the beyond", facilitating ingress into the angelic realm and the attainment of divine vision.
Chastity, therefore, functions as both a religious and a moral ideal within Persian literature, bridging the mythic and the pragmatic domains. This dual function underscores its significance in regulating sexuality, reinforcing marital bonds, and solidifying social cohesion:
Each grain you stir, a gem, a musk divine This treasure holds my heart, forever thine (Khaghani 2006, 544).
Likewise, Persian literary texts frequently portray chastity as a practice that necessitates the subjugation of natural human inclinations and desires, demanding unwavering restraint and vigilance.
This struggle against inherent impulses is often framed as a heroic endeavor, wherein individuals, armed with the "shield" of chastity, transform into metaphorical knights battling against vice within the "wilderness of life". Thus, chastity, in Persian literature, is not merely a passive state but an active, transformative force that shapes individual character and societal order.
## Enduring Friendship
The inclusion of "grave goods" within funerary rites has prompted diverse scholarly interpretations regarding their philosophical and functional significance. One perspective posits that these artifacts were provisioned for utilization within the afterlife, potentially facilitating resurrection or equipping the deceased for their postmortem journey (Härke 2014). Conversely, another interpretation suggests that these objects served as protective talismans, ensuring the deceased's security throughout their transition. Alternatively, it is argued that the bereaved may have viewed these items as extensions of the deceased's persona, embodying symbolic representations of their beliefs, experiences, or life narrative (Swan, et al. 2023).
According to an ancient Persian tradition, musk, along with other luxury items like saffron and silk, was interred with the deceased, elevating its status to that of an eternal companion:
With camphor, musk, and rose's sweet embrace, They bathed his form, a sunlit grace. His flesh, soft as the jasmine's bloom, Lay like a bud within the tomb (Savoji 2010, 595)
## Yearning for Homeland
Torn from its Tibetan homeland and severed from its ancestral lineage, the musk deer was traded far and wide. As a result, in Persian literary tradition, musk has come to symbolize the profound sorrow of exile and the yearning for one's roots, serving as a poignant symbol for the human experience of displacement and alienation:
Zephyr asked the Chinese musk, why so forlorn, Your spirit dimmed, your hope withdrawn? The musk replied, in exile's plight, I languish here, a captive blight (Savoji 2010, 599).
This symbolic association stems from the belief that musk, with its evocative fragrance, mirrors the emotional depth and nostalgic yearning of the human spirit. When poets utilize musk to symbolize homesickness, they allude to its inherent longing for its original, paradisiacal abode.
They suggest that this yearning reflects the individual's own inner journey towards selfdiscovery and spiritual peace. This concept of journey, within the framework of Persian thought, is intrinsically linked to the experiences of life, personal growth, and ultimately, a return to a state of spiritual wholeness, mirroring the journey back to the divine (Abadian 2012). For this very reason, the audience is invited to acquaint their spirits and bodies with the arduous path of learning, discipline, and human refinement:
Forsake the banquet's tempting, dainty bite, Embrace asceticism, pure and bright. The Chinese deer, through abstinence, does grow, Its musk, refined, a fragrant, noble show. Not gluttony, but self-control, the key, To virtues rare, for all eternity (Baylaghani 1991, 151).
## Cosmetic Substance
Dyes, colorful substances, have served a diverse range of functions throughout human history, from artistic expression in painting and writing to practical applications in textile dyeing and personal beautification.
In Persian literary tradition, beauty is conceptualized as a trajectory originating from internal dispositions and culminating in external manifestations (Pourrajabi and Ghobadi 2022). Over time, the application of cosmetics evolved into a ritualistic practice of self-enhancement, wherein individuals sought to effect a profound transformation and cultivate an "authentic sense of self" (McCabe, Malefyt and Fabri 2017).
Confronted with the pervasive demands of beauty culture, individuals, particularly women, employed makeup to foster self-assurance and prepare themselves for social engagement:
I praised her hair, a fragrant, musky sight, She countered, true musk, dark as night (Onsori Balkhi 1984, 243).
Within this context, musk emerged as a highly esteemed and luxurious cosmetic, symbolizing the intermediary space between internal well-being and external presentation. Therefore, women in medieval Iran utilized musk as a means of harmonizing their inner emotional states with their outward appearance, thereby bridging the subjective and objective dimensions of beauty.
Some poets have alluded to the use of musk as a cosmetic, referencing its application to hair, face, and other bodily areas:
Your youth, love's nest, in every cell resides, Though musk may dye your hair, your spirit abides (Owhadi 1983, 71).
## Cuisine
Historically, Iranians have employed musk as a flavoring agent in a wide array of culinary creations, ranging from savory dishes like stews to sweet treats such as desserts and beverages. Aromatics were incorporated into cuisine for multifaceted purposes. They not only augmented the visual presentation of the dish, thereby enhancing its aesthetic appeal, but also contributed to its overall sensory experience.
In this regard, the addition of aromatics can be considered an integral aspect of culinary artistry, akin to the decorative elements employed in other artistic media (Marin 1994). Within Persian literary traditions, comestibles infused with musk were often symbolized as ambrosia, imbued with the purported capacity to bestow longevity and revitalization. Persian poets frequently depicted these musk-enhanced foods as elixirs of life, thereby associating them with heroes and celestial realms (Abadian 2013):
On feast day four, a banquet's grand display, Rich beef, with saffron's hue and rose's spray, In aged wine and musk, it softly steeps, A tender morsel, fit for royal feasts (Ferdowsi 2009, 49)
Musk was traditionally believed to possess preservative properties, leading to the widespread practice of fumigating food storage vessels prior to use (Peterson 1980). Persians also developed a technology and processing method that enabled them to enhance the strength, color, taste, and aroma of wine (Abadian 2012).
## Monetary Unit
In China, the origin of musk, this substance was valued as highly as, if not more than, pearls. It is evident that the price of musk significantly increased in countries that imported it. Consequently, certain poets have alluded to the disparity in value, suggesting that pure musk was relatively inexpensive in China but commanded exorbitant prices in distant lands:
My verse in Persian, alas, seems cheap and slight, A worthless thing, unseen, devoid of light. Like musk in China, lost and unrefined, My words lack worth, forgotten, unaligned (Sa'di 2003, 38).
The escalating value and scarcity of musk eventually led to its adoption as a form of currency in certain regions (Abadian 2013). Accordingly, its possession became a symbol of exceptional prestige, signifying opulent wealth and elevating its owners to a status akin to divine beings, characterized by a life of ease and freedom from hardship.
Furthermore, musk acquired symbolic associations with immortality and celestial grace, representing the attainment of an idealized human existence. Thus, the transformation of musk into a valuable commodity and its subsequent symbolic elevation within Persian culture reflects its profound impact on social and economic structures, as well as its integration into the symbolic language of human aspiration and achievement.
Through these varied symbolic roles – from physical attributes like color and scent to moral and emotional allegories – musk in Persian poetry emerges as an exceptionally versatile symbol. The poets capitalized on its real-world rarity and attributes to convey messages about beauty, morality, and the human condition.
## Conclusion
By drawing on historical and cultural data from China, Iran, the broader Islamic world, the study established musk’s material and spiritual significance across contexts as a foundation for its poetic symbolism. The critical findings of this paper lie in how Persian poets transformed musk’s tangible qualities into a versatile symbolic language, reflecting and shaping Iranian social norms, values, and existential concerns. Through thematic analysis of classical poetry, musk emerges as more than an aromatic; it is a dynamic emblem of human experience.
These findings underscore musk’s role as a poetic vehicle for navigating complex sociohistorical contexts. Poets leveraged its rarity, medicinal potency, and cultural prestige – drawn from its Chinese origins, religious importance, and Iranian heritage – to critique power structures, advocate reform, and explore spiritual depths, often under courtly patronage. Musk’s symbolic density reveals a tension between compliance with dominant ideologies and subtle resistance, mirroring the poets’ dual agency. Its celestial depictions (e.g., paradise resident, divine gift) tie it to Sufi ideals of enlightenment, while its earthly uses (e.g., cosmetics, currency) ground it in daily life, bridging the mundane and transcendent. This duality positions musk as a mirror of Iranian identity – sensual yet sacred, material yet metaphysical – offering profound insights into evolving cultural values and emotional landscapes across centuries.
While this study focused on historical and symbolic dimensions, it did not address the ecological cost of musk (deer exploitation). Harvesting musk often involved the slaughter or inhumane captivity and breeding of deer. The ecological and ecolinguistic implications of this animal exploitation have yet to be explored.
This aspect remains open for future exploration, perhaps examining whether Persian literature contains any awareness or ethical commentary on the sourcing of musk.
