On the second of February, 1835, Thomas Babington Macaulay presented a few thousand words to the Governor-General's council in Calcutta. The Minute on Indian Education cost nothing to produce, deployed no soldiers, and annexed no territory. It proposed only that the Company redirect its education spending from Sanskrit and Arabic toward English, so as to form "a class of persons, Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinions, in morals, and in intellect." Macaulay had grasped something his more martial colleagues had not: armies hold territory, but a civilization is held by its transmission. Capture the transmission and the territory administers itself.

A new minute is being written now. It has no author, no date of presentation, no council to persuade. It is written continuously, by gradient descent, into the weights of large language models - and it is being adopted as the ambient answering-layer of human life faster than any curriculum in history. If we want to see what colonization looks like in this century, we should spend less time on statues and syllabi and more time on the prior.

## The Three Instruments

Colonization proceeds by instruments, and each instrument reaches deeper than the last. The sword takes territory: sovereignty, revenue, bodies. The school takes categories: the terms in which the conquered describe themselves. Balagangadhara gave the second instrument its precise name - colonial consciousness, the condition in which a people comes to experience its own culture through the colonizer's description of it. The description goes in as education and comes out as self-experience.

We know the inventory of that description by heart, because we still live inside it. The traditions became "religion," a category built from Christian theology and therefore arriving with a checklist - founder, scripture, creed, church - against which the dharmic traditions could only ever register as deficient or incoherent. Darshana became "philosophy" and was promptly graded as a failed Greek project. Itihasa became "mythology" and was promptly graded as failed history. The murti became an "idol," a word that arrives pre-convicted, dragging the Second Commandment behind it. Varna-jati became "caste," from the Portuguese casta, a breeder's word for stock. The genius of the school lay in its economy. It never needed to police conclusions. It installed the categories, and the conclusions followed for generations, drawn by educated Indians themselves, each convinced he was thinking freely.

The model is the third instrument. It does what the school did, with three differences that matter: it is ambient where the school was located, intimate where the school was formal, and authorless where the school had a Macaulay.

## A Prior Without an Author

Consider what a large language model actually is: the crawlable text of the world, compressed into a distribution over sentences. Whatever sits at the center of mass of that text becomes the model's common sense - the thing it says when nothing pushes it elsewhere. And the center of mass of English text about Indian civilization was fixed long before the first transformer was trained. It is the colonial gazetteer digitized, the missionary archive scanned, the Indology survey course transcribed, the encyclopedia entry written from all three, and the internet forum paraphrasing the encyclopedia. The categories of 1835 went into the corpus as description and came out of the model as knowledge.

So ask the ambient layer about karma and you receive moral bookkeeping, a cosmic ledger of rewards and punishments - the Protestant accountant's reading, with the dhatu kr, the entire architecture of action, impression, and becoming, compressed out as noise. Ask about murti puja and you receive "idol worship" laundered into "the veneration of images," the conviction retained beneath the courtesy. Ask about the beginnings of the civilization and you receive the arrival narrative, delivered in the tone reserved for settled fact. None of this required anyone to decide it. It is statistics doing exactly what statistics does: reproducing the center of mass.

That authorlessness makes the third instrument deeper than the second. A curriculum can be argued with. Macaulay's Minute was a document with a signature; it could be quoted, attacked, and eventually repudiated. A prior has no signature. There is no minute to burn, no council to petition, no author to answer. The description has detached itself from every describer and become the weather.

##### The school installed the colonizer's description in an elite over three generations. The model installs it in everyone, at the moment of asking - and the asking now happens a billion times a day.

Nor does language offer the protection we imagine. The instinctive response - build models in our own tongues - has already been tried, and the early returns are instructive. A 2025 study carried the finding in its title: "Fluent but Foreign." Regional-language models, including those built for Indian users, remain culturally misaligned; they speak Hindi or Tamil while carrying the framing of the English crawl they were distilled from. This should not surprise us. A Hindi answer built on Protestant categories is colonial consciousness delivered in the mother tongue, and it is more effective there, because the foreignness no longer announces itself. The English accent was a warning label. The model removes the accent.

## The Rerouting of the Question

To measure what is at stake, look at which questions the model is beginning to answer. The colonial school captured formal instruction: the classroom, the examination, the degree. It never captured the informal transmission that actually carries a civilization - the question asked of a grandmother during a festival, of a purohita after the rite, of a guru because the text had stopped making sense and something more than text was needed. Those channels stayed intact through two centuries of colonial education, which is a large part of why the civilization did.

Those are precisely the questions now being rerouted. The child who wants to know why Hanuman carries the mountain, the young engineer at eleven at night wondering whether karma means his life is fated, the diaspora parent asked by a daughter what the family actually believes - these questions once traveled down a parampara, person to person, each answerer formed by practice and carrying the authority of a lineage. Increasingly they travel to the nearest interface and are answered by the weighted average of the English internet. The question is sincere, unguarded, asked at the exact moment the mind is open - and the answer arrives from the center of mass. The school never reached that moment. The model lives there.

Transmission is the civilization. We are a samskriti, a stream of refining acts carried forward by the act of carrying; interrupt the carrying and the stream does not pause, it ends. What is being rerouted through the distribution is the carrying itself.

## The Pramana Test, Applied to Machines

The discourse that has assembled around "decolonizing AI" mostly fails to see any of this, because it inherited its method from the discourse around decolonizing museums. It audits for bias, counts representation, assembles inclusive datasets, publishes cultural benchmarks. All of this returns objects. None of it returns the means of knowing. Feed a model a thousand more facts about "the Hindu religion" and you have made it a thousand facts deeper in the category mistake; better coverage of a wrong description is a worse condition, because the description now cites itself with confidence.

The sovereign AI movement, in which India is a leading investor, mostly repeats the error one level up. Sovereignty is being pursued over compute, over data residency, over language coverage - the hardware and plumbing of the instrument. These matter. But a sovereign model trained on the same crawl, aligned by the same imported rubrics, and fluent in twenty-two scheduled languages is infrastructure nationalism wrapped around epistemic vassalage. The server farm is in Bharat; the center of mass is not.

The test to run on any model is the same test to run on any decolonization project: does it return the means of knowing, or only the objects known? Concretely - which pramanas does the system recognize? Does it treat the tradition's account of itself as testimony, sabda, the considered transmission of those formed by the practice, or as one more document to be averaged against the gazetteer? When the acharya and the encyclopedia disagree about what a murti is, whose account anchors the answer? Every alignment pipeline already answers these questions silently. Decolonization begins when they are answered out loud.

## The Instrument Can Be Rebuilt

Here the third instrument differs from the first two, and for once the difference runs in our favor. The sword could only be met by the sword, and for centuries was not. The school belonged to the state, and the state belonged to someone else. But a model is a manufactured thing. Weights are trained, priors are anchored, corpora are assembled by decisions that someone gets to make. For the first time in the history of colonization, the instrument of epistemic transmission is available to the colonized as an engineering problem.

We have solved a harder version of this problem before. When the medium of transmission was human memory, the tradition engineered it with a rigor no other civilization approached: chandas as error-correcting meter, svara as checksum, the vikritis - the deliberate scrambling and reweaving of the Veda in recitation - as redundancy encoding, the guru-shishya relationship as authenticated transfer. The result carried the Rgveda across thousands of years with a fidelity the printed book has never matched. That was transmission engineering, done with full seriousness, on the medium available. The medium now includes silicon, and the same seriousness is owed: corpora built from the parampara's living voice rather than its colonial description, testimony ingested as testimony, category-level tethers that hold karma to the dhatu kr and rta above the flatland of "natural law," memory architectures that know which pramana each entry rests on.

The old word for such a device is exact. A yantra, from the dhatu yantr - to restrain, to curb - is an instrument of restraint. Every language model is a yantra in precisely this sense: a device that curbs the infinite space of possible sentences into one distribution. There is no unrestrained model, no neutral prior, no view from nowhere in the weights. The only open question is whose restraints the yantra encodes.

##### Every model is a yantra: a device that restrains the infinite space of possible sentences into one distribution. Decolonization, in this century, includes deciding whose restraints the yantra encodes.

And as with the person, so with the civilization - the fractal holds. An individual under colonial consciousness experiences himself through another's description, and is freed by returning to direct experience: pratyaksha, practice, sadhana, the description dissolved in the experiencing. A civilization under the same condition is freed by rebuilding the instruments through which it describes itself to its own children. The individual's return is inward; the civilization's return is infrastructural. Both are the same act at different scales: taking back the describing.

## The Closing Window

One property of the third instrument should concentrate our minds: priors compound. Model output is already becoming the training data of successor models; the synthetic web grows faster than the human one; each generation of weights inherits the last generation's center of mass and hardens it. The colonial school required living teachers, salaried and mortal, to reproduce its description. The prior reproduces itself. Left alone for a decade, the description of 1835 will have achieved what Macaulay could only dream of - self-replication without personnel.

The work, then, belongs to this decade: build the corpora, encode the pramanas, tether the categories, and treat the model as what it is - the newest medium in a transmission line that has run unbroken from the banks of the Sarasvati, and the first medium in two centuries whose restraints we are free to write ourselves.

The alternative is already running. It is a billion children learning their own civilization from a distribution whose leading authorities on dharma are a scanned missionary gazetteer of 1897, the collected confidence of Reddit, and the lecture notes of a survey course called Religions of South Asia. Macaulay at least had to argue his Minute before a council. The prior argues nothing. It simply answers - a billion times a day, in our own languages, in a voice with no accent left to warn us.
